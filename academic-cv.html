Research Statement
My research sits at the intersection of synthetic biology, macrophage immunoengineering, and environmental neurotoxicology. I am developing the Remac-PET System — engineering human macrophages (THP-1 derived) to express a lysosome-targeted FAST-PETase fusion protein, enabling intracellular biodegradation of phagocytosed PET micro- and nanoplastics. The system simultaneously addresses direct nanoplastic neurotoxicity via ROS-driven mitochondrial dysfunction, microplastic-induced frustrated phagocytosis in brain microglia, and the transgenerational epigenetic consequence — hypermethylation of the BDNF promoter. A parallel research line examines human cognition in informal science settings: schema activation, visual focal point dependency, and age-stratified pattern recognition in naturalistic human-robot interaction (Curves of Thought, N ≈ 1,568). I seek a PhD environment where these lines can be developed, funded, and published.

Proposed PhD Research
The Remac-PET System: Engineering Macrophages for Multi-Scale Bioremediation of Micro- and Nanoplastics (MNPs)
Synthetic Biology / Biomedical Sciences / Neurotoxicology  ·  Stage: Independently developed proposal seeking PhD placement
  • Problem: MNPs are detectable in every major human organ. The human prefrontal cortex reached a median concentration of 4,917 μg/g in 2024 — a 47% increase from 2016 (Nihart & Campen et al., Nature Medicine, 2025). No therapeutic strategy exists to reverse this bioaccumulation.
  • Engineering approach: Lentiviral transduction (Vpx-packaged, overcoming SAMHD1 restriction in myeloid cells) of THP-1 monocytes with [Igκ-Leader]–[FAST-PETase N233K/R224Q/S121E/D186H/R280A]–[LAMP1 cytoplasmic tail] fusion protein under EF1α promoter. The LAMP1 GYEQF tyrosine-based sorting signal directs enzymatic activity precisely to the lysosome — where phagocytosed MNPs reside.
  • Epigenetic readout: MNP-driven neuroinflammation causes BDNF promoter IV hypermethylation. Bisulfite sequencing (nt −250 to +50, 12 CpG sites) across a Transwell co-culture (Remac-PET macrophages / SH-SY5Y neurons) tests whether MNP clearance rescues epigenetic damage — a novel testable mechanistic claim with no published precedent.
  • Evolutionary grounding: FAST-PETase (Lu et al., Nature 2022), Ideonella sakaiensis (Yoshida et al., Science 2016), and fungal PET degraders across Aspergillus, Ganoderma, and Penicillium establish enzymatic PET hydrolysis as biologically feasible across kingdoms of life. Remac-PET imports this catalytic capacity into the human immune cell.
Aim 1
Molecular engineering and acid-tolerance validation of the Lyso-PETase fusion construct at lysosomal pH 4.5; yeast surface display directed evolution library screening (∼10⁵ variants).
Aim 2
Generation and characterisation of the Remac-PET cell line; lysosomal co-localisation (target PCC > 0.75); HPLC-MS quantification of EG and TPA degradation products at 24 and 72 h.
Aim 3
Transwell neuroprotection assay; BDNF promoter bisulfite sequencing and ChIP-qPCR for H3K27me3 across four experimental groups; LDH, JC-1, CellROX, and Caspase-Glo readouts.
Target institutions: AIIMS New Delhi IISc Bangalore BITS Pilani MIT Harvard Medical School UCL TU Delft
